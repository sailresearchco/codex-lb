An early-reset bucket can contain only exhausted but still eligible accounts with extra credits. Restricting capacity weighting to that bucket can spend extra credits even though a later-reset account has included allowance. The new preference applies before discretionary ranking when selecting a new account. For example, an unbound request chooses an eligible account with 50% weekly allowance and a reset next week over an exhausted account resetting tomorrow.

Established conversations are deliberately different: session preferences, soft affinity, files, stored responses, and open transports retain their existing ownership behavior. This change does not add an exhaustion-triggered migration or error. An established conversation may continue using extra credits while another account has included allowance. Existing pressure and failure recovery rules can still choose replacements through their normal paths.

The classification uses normalized applicable quota windows, not effective usage inflated by local concurrency. Unknown usage remains eligible; elapsed usage windows use the existing normalization. This is a routing preference based on the latest observed usage, not a billing guarantee or authorization to redeem resets or buy credits.

Foreground admission retains the existing bounded recovery probes, including their quiet periods and reservations. Opportunistic requests keep their existing pool-dependent burn and reserve rules. The preference does not turn a temporarily unavailable account into an eligible replacement simply because it has unused allowance.
